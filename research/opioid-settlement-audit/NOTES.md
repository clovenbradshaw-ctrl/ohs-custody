# Opioid settlement funds audit -- research notes, not yet in sources.json

Source: https://www.nashville.gov/sites/default/files/2026-06/Opioid_Settlement_Funds_06102026.pdf?ct=1781119825
Fetched: 2026-09-05, direct GET, 200, application/pdf, 1,547,656 bytes.
Office of Internal Audit, "Audit of the Metropolitan Nashville Government Opioid
Settlement Funds," dated June 10, 2026. Scope: March 1, 2022 - May 31, 2025.

## What the document actually says (verbatim/close paraphrase)

- Observation A (HIGH risk): payroll allocation. Emails show the "Behavioral
  Health and Wellness Division - Program Manager" (not named in the document)
  reviewed, edited, and directed employee responses to the auditors' inquiry
  about time spent on opioid work before they were submitted. Auditors:
  "the email responses could not be relied upon and salary compliance with
  spending requirements could not be determined." ~$1.5M (51% of FY25 opioid
  spending) is payroll.
- Observation B (HIGH risk): no formal governance/oversight structure exists.
  Board of Health met 45 times in the audit period; opioid settlement activity
  came up once (March 2022). Behavioral Health and Wellness Advisory Council
  met 26 times, 7 touched opioid funds (mostly RFP process). No external body
  currently oversees spending.
- Observation C (MEDIUM): contractor invoice review was insufficient at the
  program level (Mental Health Cooperative, Samaritan Recovery Community).
  One $104,066 payment sent to the wrong vendor (caught by the vendor, later
  corrected); one Nov 2024 invoice paid twice, net later corrected; a $29,314
  vehicle capital charge went in without supporting documentation.
- Observation D (MEDIUM): OAC-required grant reports were inaccurate or not
  filed on time.
- Management Acknowledgement Letter is signed by Sanmi Areola, Director,
  Metro Public Health Department (MPHD), dated May 28, 2026. It explicitly
  states: "the report concluded that the use of the settlement funds was, and
  is, in accordance with what the Steering Committee recommended" -- i.e. the
  audit's own finding is about governance/controls/interference, not that the
  money was diverted to the wrong purposes. MPHD's response also promises
  "appropriate internal administrative actions... regarding interference by
  the program manager."

## The "Dr. Anne" identity question -- UNRESOLVED

The user's tip named "Dr. Anne" as the person who avoided public oversight on
opioid funds, sourced to the user's own recorded interview with a committee
member of "the board." The audit document itself never names the "Program
Manager" it holds responsible for interference. Web search turned up
Dr. Anidolee "Ann" Melville-Chester, PhD, Director of the Division of
Behavioral Health and Wellness at MPHD -- the exact division named in
Observation A -- as the closest match (thecontributor.org ran a Q&A profile
of her). That does NOT establish she is personally the "Program Manager" the
audit describes; a Division Director and a grant Program Manager can be, and
often are, different people. This needs a direct check (an org chart, a
records request, or asking the user's own source directly) before it goes
into any manifest entry as a named claim.
