Dear Members of the Metro Audit Committee, 
The Metro Council bears a fiduciary responsibility to ensure proper stewardship of public funds—particularly 
federal grant monies subject to strict compliance requirements—and also a fundamental duty to ensure the 
safety of Nashville's citizens, most especially its most vulnerable residents sheltered in facilities overseen by 
Metro. Council created and tasked the Office of Homeless Services (OHS) with this responsibility in regard to 
Nashville’s unhoused population.  
Since the creation of the office, serious questions have been raised regarding the leadership of OHS Director 
April Calvin, and numerous documented examples of mismanagement have been brought forth, including but 
not limited to: 
●  Financial mismanagement and/or potential fraud related to expenditures of both federal ARP and 
Metro funds as well as contract compliance; 
●  Lack of oversight of Metro facilities, including Strobel House, resulting in harm to residents and staff 
opening Metro to liability; 
●  Staffing and hiring mismanagement;  
●  Overall mismanagement resulting in the failure to improve housing outcomes despite a massive influx 
of federal and Metro dollars, and failure to deliver on its chartered obligations to coordinate Nashville's 
homelessness response through shelter management, outreach, planning council support, and 
maintaining an inventory of third-party service providers.1 
Financial Crisis 
Local journalists and advocates have reported $3.2M+ in suspected financial irregularities requiring 
immediate forensic examination: 
●  $568,923 in improper invoices to Depaul USA related to contract L-60542  
○  10 months of invoices submitted to and paid by Metro outside of the contract dates3,4 
○ 
Invoices referenced contract value of $1.2 million; actual contract value of L-6054 was 
$500,000 
●  $565,577 from Landlord Engagement funds: 
○  $500,000 allegedly misallocated, currently under internal audit by Metro 
○  $65,5775 in temporary employee expenses charged to this restricted fund although these 
temporary employees allegedly did not provide landlord engagement services 
■  $29,951 purportedly for Director Calvin’s administrative assistant 
●  $130,109 charges on a single department credit card, in addition to $46,994.146 of documented 
reimbursements to the department Director and staff 
○  Large sum of questionable expenses, including excessive expenditures at restaurants 
■  $1,800.00 billed to “WP COLEYSJJ” which appears to be Cooley’s Jook Junt, operated 
by Director Calvin’s sister  
●  $2,000,000 of temporary ARPA funds used for permanent positions and supportive housing, creating a 
future “funding cliff” that will require an injection of Metro general budget funds.7 
○  $194,097 in temporary staffing costs used to effectively circumvent Civil Service hiring rules 
Miscellaneous Finance Questions 
●  $11,000,000 in payments to service providers coded as “temporary service” for contracts that do not 
align with this definition17 
○ 
(Including Community Care Fellowship, Room In The Inn, The Salvation Army A Georgia Corporation, The Salvation 
Army Nashville Area Command, Step Up On Second Street, Inc., United Neighborhood Health Services, Park 
 
  
 
 
 
 
Center, Inc, Metro Government of Nashville & Davidson Co,  Nashville Electric Service, Mending Hearts Inc,, Colby’s 
Army Inc, The Contributor Inc, Dismas Inc, Nashville Safe Haven Family Shelter Inc, Safe Haven Family Shelter, The 
Hospitality Hub of Memphis) 
●  3,482 ledger edits totalling $8,901,763 made from 7/7/2023 - 6/30/20259  
●  Significant amounts of money moved from resolution RS2022-1697 to RS2022-1698 without an 
amendment to either resolution. Expenditures do not appear to align with Metro Finance’s Interagency 
Agreement for SLERF usage with OHS10 
●  Multiple pledges to landlords not honored in a timely manner resulting in financial hardships and 
strained relationships between tenants and landlords  
○  During a recent encampment closure, OHS approved a house with extensive documented 
code violations11 for residents to move in to; OHS then failed to pay agreed rent and deposit 
in a timely manner, resulting in letter from landlord outlining intent to evict 
Unsustainable Staffing Structure 
●  Requirement that OHS staff without proper training work double, over-time shifts at the cold weather 
shelter 
●  Circumvention of Metro HR hiring policies to hire full-time temporary staff using temporary federal 
funds, later converted to full-time staff 
Lack of Transparency Surrounding Safety Incidents at Strobel House  
Multiple incidents and conditions opening Metro government to liability due to a lack of an active contract:   
●  August 2025 fire incident that caused extensive damage to Strobel House 2nd floor, not included in 
the director's report to the Metro Council's Public Health and Safety Committee 
○  Question of the existence of an evacuation plan for wheelchair residents on upper floors 
●  October 4, 2024 stabbing at Strobel House - incident not available on public databases, and not 
reported to Public Health & Safety Committee or District 19 Council Member, despite Director Calvin 
being on-site immediately following incident12; malfunctioning security cameras13 
●  October 25, 2024 staff injury from operating equipment without training, purportedly resulting in 
chronic injury14  
Operational Collapse Despite Massive Funding Increases 
●  FY2020-2021: Under former leadership, total funding (Metro and federal funds) increased from $8.8M 
to $20.4M13, housing placements increased from approximately 975 to 1,875.15 
○  21 FTEs in 2021 
●  FY2021-2024: Despite funding more than doubling from $20.4M to $46.6M14 housing placements did 
not substantively increase (1,875 housing placements in 2021 compared to 1,912 in 2024) 
○  Federally mandated annual count of outdoor and unsheltered homeless individuals (PIT Count) 
essentially unchanged (2,016 in 2020 to 2,180 in 2025)14 
○  528% total funding increase from FY2020 with zero verified improvement14 
○  $26.2M additional annual spending producing no demonstrable increase in permanent 
housing placements14 
○  Staffing doubled from 2021 to 2025 to 43 FTEs 
●  80+ non-permanent units at Rodeway (97 Wallace Road) held empty for a year 
●  Additional units and services available but inaccessible due to a lack of timely referrals from OHS 
through the Coordinated Entry process, which landlords and service providers are required to use to fill 
units. 
●  Breakdown of partnership between OHS and private developer resulting in removal of 14 units 
of permanent housing at Shelby House; $2 million returned and residents forced to relocate 
 
 
  
 
 
●  $1,900,000 Salvation Army contract dispute with conflicting claims about scope, resulting in TSA 
terminating the contract in October 202416 
●  Overall shift of funding and prioritization of resources to temporary responses to outdoor 
homelessness over sustainable, permanent housing placements  
○  Hundreds of people displaced from encampments since 2022 with no significant increase in 
permanent housing placements 
The Office of Homeless Services is tasked with coordinating how we as a community address the 
homelessness crisis we face. Yet, to date, no significant progress has been made toward permanently housing 
our most vulnerable despite a massive increase in resources, and the relationships within the community 
central to reaching that goal have been fractured, perhaps beyond repair. 
The vulnerable deserve protection. The public deserves truth. And the law demands action. An audit at this 
time is both warranted and necessary, before further violations occur, before additional federal funds are 
compromised, and before another preventable tragedy befalls those we have sworn to protect. 
Sincerely, 
Councilmember Ginny Welsch 
ginny.welsch@nashville.gov
