We reviewed and considered all materials submitted prior to making this 
determination. The procedural history demonstrates that MHRC afforded the 
Respondent multiple opportunities over approximately ten months to provide 
information, clarify program practices, participate in informal resolution, and 
respond to investigative inquiries before the investigation proceeded to a 
probable cause determination. I considered this procedural history when 
evaluating the completeness of the investigative record. It is my determination 
as Executive Director of MHRC that this reflects sufficient inquiry as to allow an 
appropriate determination of probable cause for discrimination, or lack thereof. 
Standard for Determination 
Under MHRC Rules and Procedures, a finding of probable cause requires 
sufficient evidence to support a reasonable belief that unlawful discrimination 
has occurred. The MHRC is required to evaluate whether the evidence 
establishes a nexus between the alleged conduct and a protected class under 
Title VI, and whether the conduct rises to the level of a violation within the 
Commission's jurisdiction. 
Findings 
After a thorough review of the full record, we find that the evidence does 
establish probable cause. Specifically: 
1.  OHS administered and coordinated federally funded housing programs 
during the relevant period; 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
One city for all people 
2.  OHS selected eight participants from the Nashville Rescue Mission; 
3.  Documentation describing participant selection criteria, rental 
assistance structures, and program exit policies were requested but not 
produced during the investigation; 
4.  Participants experienced changes in rental assistance obligations 
during the course of program participation; 
5.  There were inconsistencies in how program expectations, rental 
assistance duration, and housing transition pathways were 
communicated; 
6.  Some participants did not receive housing vouchers as anticipated 
under program representations; 
7.  The affected participant population included a substantial proportion of 
Black women and women of color, consistent with known demographic 
patterns in sheltered homelessness;  
8.  Similarly situated participants experienced differing outcomes 
regarding rental assistance and housing transition supports, without 
fully documented justification; and 
9.  The affected population includes a substantial proportion of Black 
women and women of color, consistent with documented demographic 
patterns in sheltered homelessness. The challenged practices involve 
access to housing stabilization resources that disproportionately affect 
this population. 
Determination 
Based upon the Staff Investigative Report and my independent review, I hereby 
determine that there is probable cause to believe that the administration of 
federally funded housing assistance and housing stabilization programs by the 
Office of Homeless Services resulted in inconsistent application of policies and 
practices that had a disproportionate adverse effect on Black women and 
women of color, in a manner that warrants further proceedings under Rule 2.7 
of the MHRC Rules and Procedures. 
Accordingly, this complaint is upheld, and will advance to a formal conciliation 
process. 
Conciliation 
Per MHRC's rules and procedures, this complaint now advances to a formal 
conciliation process. This letter serves as our first attempt to effectuate the 
conciliation and mediated resolution of this complaint, including what steps 
must be taken to eliminate the alleged   discriminatory practice.

Closing  
We appreciate both the seriousness of the concerns raised and the continued 
engagement of all parties. While probable cause is not a finding of guilt, it is a 
higher standard than reasonable suspicion and demands a higher degree of 
certainty and fair probability that evidence of discrimination will be found. 
Nothing in this Determination constitutes a final finding of discrimination or 
liability. 
We share this only to impress the seriousness of the allegations and our 
investigative findings to date. It is still our intent to achieve a resolution through 
conciliation, and without a public hearing. We appreciate your time and 
attention to this matter.
